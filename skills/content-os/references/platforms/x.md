# X (Twitter)

Default output language: follow the idea's language; English reaches the widest audience.

## Audience & mindset
Feed scrollers deciding in under a second per post. Nobody owes you attention; the first line competes with everything else on earth. Threads are read only if tweet one earns it.

## Format & length
Single post (under 280 characters) for one sharp claim; thread (5 to 9 tweets) for an argument. One point per tweet. Every tweet must stand alone when screenshotted out of context.

## Visuals
- Optional but recommended for threads: one 16:9 image, typically a receipts image (numbers, before/after, screenshot-style evidence) attached to the receipts tweet, or a summary card on the final tweet.
- Never in tweet 1 — the existing taboo stands: media or links there suppress reach.
- Single posts usually go bare; an image must add evidence, not decoration.

## Structure norms
- Tweet 1: the hook. State the pain, the counterintuitive claim, or the result. No links, no media, no hashtags, no product name if launching.
- Middle tweets: one move each (a differentiator, a piece of evidence, an example). Under 200 characters reads best.
- A receipts tweet with numbers or a screenshot raises credibility for the whole thread.
- Final tweet: the only place for a link, plus one specific ask.

## Tone & information density
Plain words, short sentences, first person. High density: cut every word that does not carry weight. Numbers beat adjectives. Lowercase casualness is acceptable; corporate polish is not.

## Hooks & titles
The hook is a feeling the niche recognizes or a claim they want to argue with. Questions underperform statements. Specificity wins: "we cut checkout drop-off 40% by deleting a step" beats "thoughts on conversion".

## CTA norms
One ask, last tweet only: star the repo, try it, reply with X. "Repost if useful" style begging reads as spam.

## Taboos & failure modes
- A link in tweet 1 suppresses reach; the algorithm treats it as outbound spam.
- Hashtags read as 2015; skip them.
- "Excited to announce" and thread-boy formulas ("Here's what I learned. A thread 🧵") mark low-effort content.
- Threads that are one blog paragraph chopped into pieces fail; each tweet needs its own reason to exist.

## Pre-publish checklist
- Tweet 1 has no link, no media, and would stop your own scroll
- Each tweet stands alone and is under 280 characters
- Numbers or receipts appear somewhere
- Exactly one link, in the final tweet, with one specific ask
- No hashtags, no announcement clichés
