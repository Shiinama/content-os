# LinkedIn

Default output language: follow the idea's language; English reaches the widest professional audience.

## Audience & mindset
Professionals in a work mindset, scanning between tasks. They engage with content that makes them look smart to share or teaches them something applicable. The first 2 to 3 lines show before "see more"; that fold decides everything.

## Format & length
Text post, 900 to 2000 characters. One line per paragraph or very short paragraphs; whitespace is structure. Documents (carousel PDFs) suit checklists and frameworks. Long-form articles get a fraction of post reach.

## Visuals
- One 16:9 image optional; the fold is textual, so the image supports the post rather than hooks it. Keep on-image text minimal — the first three lines do the hooking.
- Document carousels (PDF) remain outside image-generation scope; when a checklist wants that format, deliver the per-page text and say so.

## Structure norms
- Lines 1 to 3: a concrete moment, result, or tension. Not the lesson itself; the reason to expand.
- Body: the story or reasoning, one thought per line, building to the insight.
- Ending: the generalized lesson plus a question or invitation that gives people an easy way to comment.

## Tone & information density
First-person professional: confident, specific, human. Share the failure before the win; earned lessons outperform advice. Medium density with narrative connective tissue. Emojis sparingly as visual bullets, not decoration.

## Hooks & titles
No titles; the first line is the hook. Working patterns: a result with numbers ("Our churn survey was lying to us. Here's how we found out"), a confession, a contrarian take on common practice. Avoid engagement-bait openers ("Agree?").

## CTA norms
A genuine question at the end outperforms "thoughts?". Links in the post body suppress reach; put links in the first comment and say so.

## Taboos & failure modes
- Broetry (dramatic one-word lines stacked for effect) reads as parody now.
- Humble-brags and fake vulnerability get called out in comments.
- Corporate announcement voice ("We are thrilled to share") kills personal-account reach.
- More than 5 hashtags looks like spam; 0 to 3 niche ones suffice.

## Pre-publish checklist
- First 3 lines create tension without giving away the lesson
- One idea, built line by line, with a specific story or number
- Link moved to first comment if any
- Ends with a question a peer could answer in one sentence
- Reads aloud like a person, not a press release
