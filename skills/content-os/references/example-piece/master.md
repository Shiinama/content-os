# Checkout conversion isn't a friction problem, it's a framing problem

We found it by accident. While debugging an unrelated layout bug in the checkout funnel, one cohort split jumped out: users who applied a coupon converted at 8.1%. Everyone else: 4.0%. Same week, same traffic, roughly 3.4k sessions.

The obvious read is selection bias: deal-seekers were going to buy anyway. Maybe. But the week-over-week drop that started this investigation coincided with the coupon banner being removed from the cart page, which points the other way: the act of applying a coupon does something to the buyer, not just the price.

Here is the claim: applying a coupon reframes the purchase as a win. The buyer stops evaluating whether the product is worth the money and starts protecting a deal they found. That is a different mental state, and it converts at twice the rate.

If that holds, the standard playbook is aimed at the wrong variable. We keep sanding friction off the flow (fewer fields, faster loads, cleaner UI) while the bigger lever might be manufacturing the deal-finding feeling for every buyer, not just the ones who arrived with a code.

The honest gap: this needs a holdout test before anyone reprices anything. Auto-applied "you found a deal" discount vs. a visible coupon field vs. control. If the auto-applied arm wins, framing beats friction and the checkout roadmap should change.
